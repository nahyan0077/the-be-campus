How Asha Went from Homemaker to Freelance Brand Designer Earning ₹60,000/Month — at 32, with Zero Design Experience

Asha Mohammed
Creative Design Graduate · Freelance Brand Designer · January 25, 2025 · 5 min read

I want to start with something honest: when I enrolled at Be Campus Academy at 32, I felt like I was too old to start something new. My children were in school. I hadn't worked professionally in six years. I had never used Adobe Photoshop in my life. I cried on the drive home after my first counselling session because I was so scared of failing.

Fourteen months later, I am a freelance brand designer earning ₹60,000 a month, with clients in Kochi, Bangalore, and even one in Dubai. I work from home, on my own schedule, during my children's school hours. This is my story — and I want every woman in Malappuram who feels like it's too late to read it carefully.

Before Be Campus: A Life on Hold

I did my BA in Malappuram and got married at 24. I stopped working after my first child. By the time my younger child started school and I had time and mental space to think about myself again, I was 31 — and completely uncertain about what I could do professionally. I had a gap in my resume. I didn't have a technical degree. And I felt like the world had moved on without me.

I knew I was creative. I had always enjoyed designing things at home — birthday invitations for my children's parties, layouts for our building's notice board, decorations. My husband kept saying "you should do something with this." I didn't know what that something was.

Why Design? Why Be Campus?

A neighbour in Kottakkal whose daughter had done a course at Be Campus suggested I visit. I went expecting another basic computer institute that would teach me MS Office. What I found was completely different.

The counsellor showed me a portfolio of work done by recent students — real brand identities, Instagram pages for businesses, packaging designs. She explained the Creative Design & Brand Building diploma in detail: Adobe suite, Figma, Canva Pro, AI design tools, and most importantly, a portfolio-based approach where we work on real brand projects through Be Cubez, the academy's agency.

She told me something I still remember: "We don't care that you're 32 and haven't worked in six years. We care that you're creative and committed. If you are, this course will give you skills that employers and clients will pay for — regardless of your age or background." I enrolled the following week.

The First Three Months

I won't pretend the beginning was easy. Photoshop has a steep learning curve and the first few weeks felt overwhelming. I was in class with students who were 19 and 20 years old and seemed to pick things up faster than me. There were moments of genuine self-doubt.

But the trainers at Be Campus were patient in a way I had not expected. They stayed after hours to help me. They explained concepts in multiple ways until I understood. And I started to notice something: while the younger students were faster at following instructions, I had something they didn't — real-world context. When we studied brand identity, I understood what it meant for a business because I had lived in the real world longer. That became my advantage.

By month three, I was producing work I was proud of. I designed a full brand identity for a fictional coffee shop as a class project and my trainer told me it was portfolio-worthy. That was the first moment I thought: maybe I can actually do this.

The Be Cubez Turning Point

The game-changer was Be Cubez — Be Campus's in-house digital marketing and design agency. In month four, our class was assigned to work on a real client project through Be Cubez: a brand refresh for a small clothing boutique in Malappuram. Real client, real brief, real feedback.

I worked harder on that project than on anything else in the course. I produced three logo concepts, a colour palette, typography guidelines, and mock-ups of the boutique's social media posts. When the client approved my design direction, I genuinely cried. Not because I was sad — because I finally understood that my work had real-world value.

Over the next two months, I worked on two more Be Cubez projects — a food packaging design for a local brand and social media creatives for a healthcare clinic. By the time I completed the diploma, I had eight real portfolio pieces with actual client approval histories. Not mock projects. Not made-up briefs. Real work.

"When I sat down for client meetings and showed my portfolio, the fact that these were real approved projects for real businesses made an enormous difference. Clients could see my work in context. They could see that real clients had trusted me. That is worth more than any certificate."

— Asha Mohammed, Freelance Brand Designer

Getting My First Paying Client

I decided early that I wanted to freelance, not get a corporate job. My family responsibilities meant I needed location flexibility. Be Campus's placement team was supportive of this decision and helped me set up a profile on Behance and reach out to local businesses directly.

My first paid project came from a bakery in Kottakkal that had seen my work through Be Cubez. They asked me to design their brand identity and packaging. I charged ₹8,000 — my first professional income in six years. It was the most significant ₹8,000 I had ever earned.

Building a Freelance Practice

After that first project, referrals started coming. One satisfied client told another. I joined a few local business WhatsApp groups and shared my work. I built a proper Instagram portfolio account. Within six months, I had a steady flow of projects — mostly brand identity, packaging design, and social media creative packages for local businesses in Malappuram and increasingly from clients in Kochi and Bangalore who found me through Instagram.

The AI tools I learned at Be Campus — particularly MidJourney for concept generation and Adobe Firefly for image work — allowed me to deliver projects faster without compromising quality. What used to take three days, I can often complete in one. This means I can take on more clients and earn more without working longer hours.

What I Earn Now

I want to be transparent here, because I know it matters to women considering this path. My current monthly income from freelance brand design is between ₹55,000 and ₹70,000 — averaging around ₹60,000. I work roughly 5–6 hours a day, five days a week, during school hours. I haven't missed a single school pickup or parent-teacher meeting.

I have three retainer clients — businesses that pay me a monthly fee for ongoing creative work — and take on project-based work from new clients as capacity allows. By this time next year, I'm planning to cross ₹1 lakh per month and potentially take on a junior designer to help.

My Message to Women in Malappuram

If you are a woman in Malappuram — a housewife, a career-break returner, a young graduate unsure of your path — and you feel like it's too late, too risky, or too uncertain to try something new, I want you to know: I felt exactly the same way at 32. I was wrong.

Be Campus will not judge your age, your gap, your background, or your confidence level on day one. They will teach you real skills, give you real project experience, and support you genuinely until you find your footing. What you do with those skills — whether that's a corporate job, freelancing, or something entirely your own — is up to you.

The only thing I wish I had done differently is start sooner. Don't make the same mistake.
