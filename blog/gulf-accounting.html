Gulf Accounting Jobs 2025: What Kerala Candidates Must Know Before Applying — and How to Actually Get Hired

Every year, thousands of BCom and MBA Finance graduates from Kerala apply for accounting jobs in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Kuwait. Every year, a significant portion of them fail at the interview stage — not because they're not intelligent or hardworking, but because their qualifications don't match what Gulf employers actually need.

The gap between Indian accounting education and Gulf employer requirements is specific, identifiable, and — most importantly — bridgeable. This article explains exactly what Gulf companies are looking for in 2025 and what Kerala candidates must do to qualify.

The Gulf Accounting Opportunity for Kerala Candidates

The Gulf has always been a significant employment destination for Kerala's professional workforce. But the accounting opportunity in 2025 is particularly strong for a specific reason: the UAE's introduction of corporate tax in June 2023.

For the first time in UAE history, most businesses are now subject to a 9% corporate tax and must maintain compliant accounting records, file tax returns, and demonstrate financial reporting standards. This has created an immediate and sustained demand for qualified accountants who understand both Gulf compliance requirements and international accounting standards.

Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 transformation, Qatar's post-World Cup economic diversification, and Kuwait's ongoing infrastructure programmes have similarly created accounting demand. The window for Kerala candidates — who have a historical trust relationship with Gulf employers and a strong cultural fit — is wide open right now.

What Gulf Companies Actually Look For

Based on our placement partner network in the Gulf and the interview feedback from our AI-ADIAT graduates, here is what Gulf accounting employers are consistently screening for in 2025:

  • IFRS knowledge — Not just awareness, but the ability to explain and apply specific standards
  • Cloud accounting software — QuickBooks Online or Xero proficiency is asked about in nearly every Gulf accounting interview
  • UAE VAT / GCC VAT — Understanding of VAT registration, filing, and FTA compliance
  • Financial reporting — Ability to prepare and interpret P&L, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statements under IFRS
  • Advanced Excel — Financial modelling, pivot tables, VLOOKUP, and dashboard creation are standard expectations
  • Multi-currency accounting — Understanding of foreign currency transactions, exchange rate treatment
Reality Check

A 2024 survey of Gulf-based HR managers with Kerala hiring networks found that over 70% of Kerala accounting applicants fail the technical screening stage — primarily due to lack of IFRS knowledge and cloud accounting software experience. This is a skills gap problem, not an intelligence problem.

IFRS: The Most Critical Qualification Gap

International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) are the accounting language of the Gulf. Every listed company, most large private companies, and increasingly mid-size businesses in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar prepare their financial statements under IFRS.

Indian accounting education teaches Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) — which are converged with but not identical to full IFRS. The differences matter in practice: IFRS 16 lease accounting, IFRS 15 revenue recognition from contracts, IFRS 9 financial instruments treatment — these are tested in Gulf accounting interviews and form the basis of day-to-day accounting work at international firms.

Our AI-ADIAT program dedicates two full months to IFRS — not as a theoretical overview, but as practical application. Students practice preparing IFRS-compliant financial statements, performing IFRS 16 lease calculations, and applying IFRS 15 revenue recognition principles to real scenarios.

Cloud Accounting: QuickBooks & Xero

The Gulf's accounting ecosystem has moved decisively to cloud-based platforms. QuickBooks Online and Xero together account for the majority of cloud accounting deployments across SMEs in the UAE — the sector with the highest volume of accounting job openings for fresh hires from Kerala.

Candidates who can demonstrate hands-on QuickBooks and Xero proficiency — including setting up company accounts, managing Chart of Accounts, processing payroll, reconciling bank accounts, and generating reports — immediately stand out in Gulf interviews. This software proficiency is the single fastest way to differentiate your CV from the hundreds of other Kerala accounting graduates applying for the same role.

Interview Preparation
Gulf accounting interviewers frequently ask candidates to walk through how they would set up a new company in QuickBooks, or how they would process a specific VAT transaction. Hands-on software practice — not just theoretical knowledge — is what gets you through these screens.

UAE VAT Knowledge Is Non-Negotiable

Since the UAE implemented VAT in January 2018, understanding UAE VAT — registration thresholds, standard and zero-rated supplies, input tax credit claims, and quarterly FTA return filing — has been a basic expectation for any accounting hire in the UAE market.

Our AI-ADIAT program covers UAE VAT comprehensively — including the nuances of the FTA's tax return process, the treatment of exempt supplies, the documentation requirements for VAT audits, and the specific scenarios (real estate, financial services, healthcare) where VAT treatment differs from standard. Candidates who can speak confidently about UAE VAT in interviews are demonstrating both knowledge and genuine market readiness.

AI Accounting Tools in Gulf Firms

The largest accounting and professional services firms in the Gulf — Big 4, regional audit firms, and large corporate finance functions — are actively implementing AI-powered accounting tools. AI-assisted reconciliation, automated financial report generation, predictive cash flow analysis, and intelligent expense categorisation are all being deployed in Gulf finance departments.

Our AI-ADIAT program teaches students how these tools work, how to use them, and — critically — how to speak about them in interviews in a way that demonstrates you are a forward-looking accountant, not someone who will be threatened by automation. Being AI-literate is a competitive advantage in Gulf accounting hiring right now.

Salary Ranges in 2025

To give you realistic expectations: entry-level accounting roles in the UAE for Kerala graduates typically start between AED 2,500–3,500 per month (approximately ₹55,000–₹77,000). Mid-level accountants with 2–3 years experience and IFRS/cloud accounting skills can command AED 4,500–7,000 (₹99,000–₹1.55 lakh). Senior accountants and financial controllers in larger firms earn AED 8,000–15,000 and above.

Be Campus AI-ADIAT graduates entering the Gulf market in 2024–2025 have been starting at AED 2,800–3,500 based on placement feedback — demonstrating that the qualification genuinely positions candidates at the stronger end of the entry-level range.
